# World Health Organization (WHO) Global Air Quality Guidelines (2021)

Source URL: https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240034228
Retrieval Date: 2026-11-15

## Executive Summary
In 2021, the WHO published updated global air quality guidelines providing evidence-based recommendations on pollutant concentrations to protect public health. Air pollution is one of the biggest environmental threats to human health, causing an estimated 7 million premature deaths annually worldwide.

## Guideline Levels vs Interim Targets
For fine particulate matter (PM2.5), the WHO recommended levels are:
- **Annual mean guideline level:** 5 µg/m³
- **24-hour mean guideline level:** 15 µg/m³ (not to be exceeded more than 3-4 days per year)
- **Interim Target 1 (IT-1):** 24-hour mean of 75 µg/m³
- **Interim Target 2 (IT-2):** 24-hour mean of 50 µg/m³
- **Interim Target 3 (IT-3):** 24-hour mean of 37.5 µg/m³
- **Interim Target 4 (IT-4):** 24-hour mean of 25 µg/m³

For Nitrogen Dioxide (NO2):
- **Annual mean guideline level:** 10 µg/m³
- **24-hour mean guideline level:** 25 µg/m³

## Key Health Effects
Long-term and acute exposure to PM2.5 and NO2 leads to:
1. Cardiovascular diseases including ischemic heart disease and stroke.
2. Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) and asthma attacks.
3. Systemic inflammation, impaired childhood lung development, and low birth weight.
4. Lung cancer and acute lower respiratory infections.
